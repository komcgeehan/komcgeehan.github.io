2023 National Youth Risk Behavior Survey

Social Media & Student Well-Being

How is social media use associated with persistent sadness or hopelessness among U.S. high school students?

This visualization explores the overall pattern, differences between female and male students, and where those differences are largest.

Explore the data

Compare reported sadness or hopelessness across levels of social media use, examine differences between female and male students, and identify where those differences are largest.

View 1 · Overall Pattern

Sadness or Hopelessness by Social Media Use

Reported persistent sadness or hopelessness varies across social media use levels rather than following a simple, steady progression.

Why show all eight categories? The pattern is not uniformly increasing across social media use levels. Keeping the full response scale preserves variation that would be hidden by a simple low-versus-high comparison.

View 2 · Comparison by Sex

How the Pattern Differs by Sex

The overall pattern changes when the data are separated by sex. Each vertical line connects female and male students at the same level of social media use.

What stands out: Female students report higher levels of persistent sadness or hopelessness than male students across every social media use category shown.

View 3 · Size of the Gap

Where the Female–Male Gap Is Largest

The size of the difference is not constant. Ranking the gap makes it easier to see where reported sadness or hopelessness differs most.

Largest observed gap: among students who reported using social media about once an hour, the difference was 32.4 percentage points.

View 4 · Human-Centered Detail

What the Difference Looks Like Among Students

This final view focuses on students who reported using social media more than once an hour and translates the weighted percentages into a more human-readable form.

How to Read These Findings

These results show associations, not causation. The 2023 National Youth Risk Behavior Survey is cross-sectional and based on students' self-reported responses. The percentages shown here are weighted estimates intended to represent U.S. high school students. Some survey items also contain missing responses.

About This Project

This project began with sketches and a simple bar-chart pilot before evolving into four coordinated views. The final design combines traditional statistical charts with a human-centered 100-dot grid. The visualization was built with Vega-Lite, D3, HTML, and CSS.

Source: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, 2023 National Youth Risk Behavior Survey.
