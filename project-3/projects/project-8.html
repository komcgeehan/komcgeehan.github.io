Donor-centric language turns stewardship into recognition
"Thank YOU for supporting girls like Wane" puts the donor in the headline — not Plan, not Wane. Good stewardship copy makes the donor feel like the protagonist of the story, not a bystander to it.
A specific, unexpected outcome builds donor trust
Hydroponic farming in a school greenhouse is a concrete, surprising detail — the kind donors remember. Vague impact claims erode trust; this kind of specificity earns it back and keeps donors invested in what happens next.
The sign-off builds community, not just gratitude
"We wouldn't have achieved any of this without people like you" positions the donor as part of a collective — not a transactional contributor. That shift from "thank you for giving" to "you made this possible" is what turns one-time donors into long-term supporters.

Brief

A mid-campaign stewardship email sent to active donors and lapsed OTG supporters during Plan International USA's Extreme Heat campaign, showing the on-the-ground impact of their gifts during Zambia's worst drought in decades.

Approach

Centered the email on a single, specific story — Wane and her school's hydroponic greenhouse — to give donors a tangible outcome rather than a generic impact statement. Donor-first language throughout keeps the stewardship feeling personal and earned rather than formulaic.
